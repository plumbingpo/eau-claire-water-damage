Crawl Space Water Removal: What Eau Claire Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
Our Process

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Settling Under the House

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Air From Below

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.
